    Does fertility yoga help to get pregnant? What the research and the tradition say

    If you are looking at yoga to get pregnant, start here. Many people who practice yoga when trying to conceive say it changes how their body and their month feel, and generations of women have sworn by it. Researchers are now studying why. Here is where Shvasa fertility yoga fits, pillar by pillar.

    A steadier cycle, and steadier ovulation Encouraging

    A regular cycle is usually a sign of regular ovulation, and ovulation is what conception depends on. This is where yoga has been studied most closely. Randomized trials in women with PCOS, a leading cause of irregular ovulation, have measured hormone levels and menstrual patterns after a yoga program.12 The trials are small, so we call the results encouraging, not settled. Shvasa fertility yoga is built around the part you control: a steady routine, week after week.

    Healthier sperm Promising

    A review of the yoga and male fertility research concluded that regular yoga practice improves sperm count and motility.3 In a 12-week study of infertile men, sperm function improved and oxidative stress and DNA damage in sperm fell after a yoga program.4 The studies are small and that one had no comparison group, and the direction is consistent.

    Stress, mood and staying the course Encouraging

    A 2020 systematic review of 12 studies found that mind-body programs, including yoga, were linked to lower anxiety and depression in women having fertility treatment, and reported a possible improvement in pregnancy rate.5 Fertility care asks a lot of you. A practice that gives you a place to put the stress helps you keep going.

    Rooted in centuries of practice

    Long before fertility clinics, physicians in ancient India wrote about how conception happens and how to prepare for it. Ayurveda's classical texts, the Charaka Samhita and the Sushruta Samhita, describe four factors that must come together: ritu, the right time in the cycle; kshetra, the health of the reproductive organs; ambu, nourishment; and beeja, the egg and the sperm.6 They treat preparing the body before conception, for both partners, as a discipline of its own, called garbhadhana.

    Yoga grew in the same soil. It works through a steady body, regulated breath and a settled mind, and Shvasa teachers draw on it every day. This is a tradition of practice and observation, not a set of clinical trials, and we say so. It is also a tradition generations of women and families have trusted, and modern research is now looking at the same ideas: cycles, ovulation, healthy sperm and the body's response to stress.

    What your teacher sees: the body behind the breath

    Our teachers are trained in human anatomy, so every cue has a reason. Scroll through four parts of the body your teacher works with in a fertility class. The picture is a simple sketch, not a medical diagram. anatomy_copy_review

    1. The diaphragm

    A dome-shaped muscle under your lungs. When you breathe in it moves down, and when you breathe out it moves back up. Slow belly breathing is taught first because it ties breath to a body that is settling, and many people find it calming.

    2. The lower back

    Long hours of sitting leave the lower back tight. Cat-cow and gentle spinal movement take each vertebra through a small, easy range, with the breath leading the movement.

    3. The hips

    The hip joints and inner thighs hold a lot of tension. Supported poses such as reclined bound angle let the hips open without forcing, with pillows or blocks under the knees.

    4. The pelvic floor

    A sling of muscles from the pubic bone to the tailbone. Teachers cue awareness of both gripping and letting go, because many people hold more tension here than they realise. This is taught gently and adapted to you.

    Fertility yoga poses and breath practices

    People ask for the best fertility yoga poses, the best fertility yoga, and simple fertility yoga exercises, and female fertility yoga in particular. There is no single best one. These are the practices people ask about most. Each one is chosen for what it does in the body, and in class a teacher adapts it to you. They sit beside your clinic's advice, not in place of it.

    Supta baddha konasana (reclined bound angle)

    A supported, lying-down pose with the soles of the feet together and knees falling out to the sides, often with pillows or blocks under the knees. Teachers use it to open the inner thighs and hips, areas that hold a lot of tension, and to let the breath settle.

    Skip or adapt if: you have knee, hip or groin pain, or a doctor has told you to avoid it. Support the knees so nothing strains.

    Bee breath yoga (bhramari pranayama)

    You breathe in through the nose and hum softly as you breathe out. The hum gives your attention something to follow and slows the breath, which many people find calming. It is a relaxation tool, not a fertility technique.

    Skip or adapt if: it makes you dizzy or uncomfortable. Keep the breath easy and never strain.

    Legs up the wall (viparita karani)

    You lie on your back with your legs resting up a wall. It is a supported inversion that many people find restoring, and teachers also use it to ease tired legs and a tight lower back.

    Skip or adapt if: you have glaucoma, uncontrolled high blood pressure or other conditions where your doctor advises against inverted positions. Ask your teacher for a version with the hips on a pillow instead.

    Cat-cow, child's pose and gentle hip openers

    Slow, easy movement for the spine and hips, linked to the breath. Useful to release a stiff back and to move without pushing.

    Skip or adapt if: anything pinches or hurts. Ask your teacher for props.

    Kapalbhati (kapalabhati, skull-shining breath) and surya namaskar

    These are more energetic. Fast, forceful breath practices such as kapalabhati, and a brisk surya namaskar, are commonly avoided once you are pregnant. Because you may be pregnant in the two weeks after ovulation without knowing it, many teachers choose gentler breath and slower sun salutations in the second half of the cycle. teacher_to_confirm

    If you practice these, ask your teacher about adapting them.

    Poses are explained for understanding, not as a prescription. Daily movement in our program is guided by your teacher.

    Frequently asked questions

    Does fertility yoga help you get pregnant?

    Many women and men find it a real support. Studies in women with PCOS have looked at hormone levels and menstrual patterns, a review of male fertility research concluded that regular yoga improves sperm count and motility, and a review of treatment studies found lower anxiety and depression and a possible improvement in pregnancy rate. The studies are small, and the tradition behind the practice is centuries old. No one can promise a pregnancy, and yoga works best beside medical care.

    Can yoga regulate my periods? Is there yoga for irregular periods?

    A regular cycle is a sign of regular ovulation, and that is what yoga programs for PCOS have been studied for. Trials have measured menstrual patterns and hormone levels after a regular practice. Results are encouraging, and the trials are small. A steady, teacher-led routine is the part Shvasa gives you. If your periods are irregular, ask your doctor to check why, and practice while you do.

    Is there yoga to conceive fast?

    Yoga is not a shortcut, and it is not meant to be one. It supports the things conception depends on, such as a regular cycle, healthy sperm and a body that is not carrying tension, month after month. People who practice regularly tend to get the most from it. If you have been trying for a while, see our next-step guide above.

    Can you increase AMH by yoga?

    We have not found a study showing that yoga raises AMH, and AMH is a marker, not a switch. It also does not tell you whether you will conceive. Yoga supports the rest of the picture: your cycle, your stress, your body. Talk to your doctor about what your result means.

    Is there fertility yoga for implantation, or yoga for uterus lining?

    No trial has shown that yoga improves implantation or the thickness of the uterine lining, so we do not claim it. What teachers focus on is the hips, the pelvic floor and the back, and the breath. If you are in treatment, follow your clinic's plan and bring your practice with you.

    Is there yoga for conceiving twins?

    No. There is no evidence for this. A healthy pregnancy is the goal, and yoga helps you get ready for it.

    Is there yoga for fallopian tubes, or yoga for uterus and ovaries?

    Tubes, ovaries and the uterus are assessed with medical tests, and yoga does not replace them. A practice keeps the hips, pelvis and back mobile and supports you through the process, and your doctor tells you what the tests show.

    What are good yoga poses during 2ww, the two-week wait, or yoga during ovulation?

    Restorative poses, slow breathing, visualization and a daily class give the wait a structure. Fast, forceful breathing exercises are commonly avoided when you might be pregnant. Your Shvasa teacher can adapt your practice to the day of your cycle.

    Is it OK to do surya namaskar while trying to conceive, or surya namaskar after ovulation? What about kapalbhati while trying to conceive?

    Gentle, slower sun salutations suit many people. Fast, forceful breath practices, including kapalbhati, are commonly avoided once pregnant, and you may not know yet in the two weeks after ovulation. Ask your teacher about adapting them, and follow your doctor's advice.

    Is yoga safe during IVF?

    Gentle practice fits most stages, and the details depend on your protocol. Ask your clinic before any strong movement, twisting or heat. Our IVF stage guide above shows what teachers focus on at each stage.

    Can men do fertility yoga?

    Yes, and we encourage it. A review of male fertility research concluded regular yoga improves sperm count and motility. It is also a way to share the journey with a partner. Partners are welcome at Shvasa.

    Does stress cause infertility?

    Professional guidance does not list stress as a proven cause of infertility.11 Stress does make the process harder to live through, and that is where a practice and a community help most.

    Is there a yoga for fertility success story I can read?

    We will share real member stories, with permission, as the program opens. Each is one person's experience.

    Is fertility yoga for beginners? Do I need experience?

    Yes, it is for beginners. No experience is needed. Classes are gentle, a teacher adapts the practice, and props such as pillows and a wall are enough.

    Can I practice while seeing a doctor?

    Yes, and it helps to do both. See your doctor for tests and treatment, and use Shvasa for the daily practice and the community around it.
